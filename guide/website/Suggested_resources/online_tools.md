# Online tools for creating DMPs

A range of online tools is available to help researchers and data stewards create, publish and manage data management plans (DMPs). These tools offer different features, including funder-specific templates, guided questionnaires, collaborative editing and export options. Some also support the export of machine-readable formats, such as JSON. The benefits of machine-actionable DMPs are discussed in the relevant section of this guide.

The tools listed below vary in functionality, access conditions and intended audiences. You can therefore choose the one that best suits your needs and institutional context.

## DMPonline

[DMPonline](https://dmponline.dcc.ac.uk/) is a web-based tool developed by the Digital Curation Centre (DCC) to help researchers create, review and share DMPs using online templates. The service is free to use and requires users to sign in to create and manage their plans. Users who are not signed in can browse publicly available DMPs.

The platform provides institutional and funder-specific templates and allows users to share their plans publicly or restrict access, including to members of their institution where supported. Institutional accounts can be linked to DMPonline accounts, allowing institutions to customise templates and provide tailored guidance to their researchers. DMPs can also be exported in different formats, including HTML and JSON.

## DMPTool

[DMPTool](https://dmptool.org/) is a free platform for creating and sharing DMPs. Users must sign in to create a new plan, while publicly available DMPs can be browsed without signing in.

The platform offers guided workflows and funder-specific templates, primarily for US funding agencies, although it can also be used by researchers working in other countries. DMPs can be exported in different formats, including HTML and JSON.

## Data Stewardship Wizard (DSW)

[Data Stewardship Wizard](https://ds-wizard.org/) is an open-source platform for creating and managing DMPs through structured questionnaires and configurable knowledge models. It supports collaborative planning and can be customised to meet different research data management requirements.

Organisations can deploy and maintain their own DSW instances. Alternatively, users can access a hosted version, with a free option for up to ten users and paid options for larger-scale use, subject to the provider's current terms. DSW can be particularly useful for research support professionals and institutions seeking to customise their DMP workflows and integrate machine-actionable approaches into research data management.

## ARGOS

[ARGOS](https://argos.openaire.eu/portal/) is a platform maintained by OpenAIRE to support the creation and management of machine-actionable DMPs (maDMPs). It is built on OpenCDMP, described in a later section of this guide.

ARGOS offers free access for individual researchers, alongside paid subscription options for teams and institutions. Users can invite collaborators to work together on the same DMP and export plans in different formats, including JSON and XML, as well as human-readable formats.

Users can publish their plans through the ARGOS repository or deposit them in Zenodo, where a DOI can be assigned. A key aim of the OpenAIRE ecosystem is to connect DMPs with research outputs, services and infrastructures through the OpenAIRE Research Graph. ARGOS supports this approach by allowing users to link information in their plans to relevant research products and resources available through the graph.

## DAMAP

[DAMAP](https://damap.org/) is an open-source tool for creating DMPs, with its source code and development resources available on [GitHub](https://github.com/damap-org).

The content and structure of DAMAP are based on Science Europe's Core Requirements for Data Management Plans. The tool guides users through the main sections of a DMP in ten steps, using questions, suggested text and supporting information. It generates a document that can be customised and used when submitting a DMP to European and national research funders.

DAMAP is also compatible with the Research Data Alliance's recommendations for machine-actionable DMPs and supports JSON export. Access to an online instance and its templates may require an account; contact the service provider for information about availability and access arrangements.

## ezDMP

[ezDMP](https://ezdmp.org/) is a web-based tool for creating DMPs. Its [GitHub repository](https://github.com/ezdmp/ezDMP-Site) provides access to the application's source code, intended for deployment and running of a self-hosted instance.

## Research Data Management Organiser (RDMO)

[Research Data Management Organiser (RDMO)](https://rdmorganiser.github.io/en/) is open-source software that supports the planning and organisation of research data management throughout the research lifecycle.

RDMO is a web-based application that can be downloaded free of charge from [GitHub](https://github.com/rdmorganiser/rdmo). However, it is not simply an online service that users can access by visiting the repository. It must be installed and configured on a server, with user accounts and access typically managed centrally by the host institution or organisation.

### OpenCDMP

[OpenCDMP](https://opencdmp.github.io/) is an open-source platform for creating and managing data management plans. It provides the underlying technology used by ARGOS and builds on the concepts of OpenDMP (Open Data Management Planning).

The platform is developed by Communication & Information Technologies Experts S.A. (CITE), with support from OpenAIRE AMKE. It offers documentation to help users understand and deploy the software.

As an open-source project, OpenCDMP can be installed and customised by organisations with the appropriate technical resources. Its source code is available on [GitHub](https://github.com/OpenCDMP/OpenCDMP), and further information can be found in the [OpenCDMP documentation](https://opencdmp.github.io/).
